Professional profile

Where research meets strategy

My work bridges the gap between deep user research and organizational strategy, creating efficient, accessible, user-centered solutions that empower people and drive businesses forward. It's the place where good insight either becomes a shipped product or quietly dies in a deck — and I've spent twenty years learning how to make it the former.

Driven by curiosity and shaped by a collaborative product-thinking mindset, I excel at transforming highly complex challenges into seamless, intuitive, and sustainable digital products.

Hierarchy is an argument. Fine Arts taught me that; enterprise software made me prove it.

I got here by way of a Fine Arts degree and a lot of hands-on front-end code, which turns out to be a useful combination: I can make the argument visually, prototype the interaction logic myself in Figma or Axure, and then sit in the sprint ceremony to make sure it survives contact with engineering.

Most of my last decade has been in healthcare and enterprise, where the stakes are real and the systems are old. Medicare sales agents, customer service advocates, healthcare providers — people doing hard jobs under time pressure on software that rarely helps them. Designing well for them is the most satisfying work I've done.

These days I'm especially interested in AI interfaces that earn trust rather than assume it. The real design work isn't the model — it's giving people enough context to decide whether to act on what it hands them.

Philosophy & approach

Six things I actually believe

01

Simplicity as a goal

Always searching for the simplest, most direct path to meaningful impact. If the solution needs a diagram to explain itself to the team, the user will need one too.

02

Collaboration first

Fostering high-performance teams and working cross-functionally to ensure alignment with both real-world user needs and broader organizational goals.

03

Continuous iteration

Relying on data-backed user insights and systematic prototyping to continually refine and elevate the experience. Rough and validated beats polished and assumed.

04

Accessibility isn't a phase

WCAG AA goes into the product lifecycle at the start, not the audit at the end. It's cheaper, kinder, and it removes usability roadblocks nobody flagged as accessibility issues.

05

Mentorship as multiplication

I've been a functional manager for international teams — strategic mentorship, honest performance guidance, and career pathways people can actually see themselves on.

06

Speak executive, think user

I present and defend design decisions to senior stakeholders in the language of roadmaps and outcomes, then go straight back to the research that justified them.
